# Emergence — structured overview of types and definitions across disciplines

Research document, opened 2026-09-09. First deliverable of the emergence round: the map of what "emergence" means, everywhere it is used — the definitional landscape a position must be earned against. Descriptive throughout; no SPLectrum-side reading here. Sources: SEP "Emergent Properties," IEP "Emergence," the primary statements named per entry.

---

## 1. The definitional axes (the cuts every definition takes a position on)

Every definition in the literature can be located by where it stands on five axes. The axes are the field's own; disputes about emergence are very often disputes about which axis matters.

- **Epistemic vs ontological.** Is emergence a fact about *our knowledge* (unpredictability, non-derivability, surprise — could be dissolved by better theory) or about *the world* (genuinely novel properties/powers, irreducible whatever we come to know)? The deepest axis; most cross-talk in the literature traces to it.
- **Weak vs strong.** Chalmers's cut: **weak** = unexpected given the low-level principles, but deducible in principle (often only by simulation); **strong** = not deducible even in principle from the low-level domain. Strong emergence is where "further fact" claims live; Chalmers holds consciousness as the only serious candidate.
- **Synchronic vs diachronic.** **Synchronic** (Broad; philosophy of mind's home case): the emergent is co-present with its base, here and now — mind on brain. **Diachronic**: the emergent arises *over time* — new properties instantiated for the first time in a process (origin of life, evolution, development). The sciences mostly traffic in diachronic emergence; philosophy of mind in synchronic — a standing source of mutual incomprehension.
- **Property vs entity vs law.** What emerges: properties (the standard case), new *entities/individuals* (Humphreys's fusion; contested), or new *laws/regularities* (the British Emergentists' "trans-ordinal laws")?
- **Predictability vs derivability vs causal novelty.** The criterion doing the work: failure of prediction (epistemic), failure of derivation/deduction (logical), or novel causal powers — with **downward causation** (the whole constraining/acting on its parts) as the strongest and most contested causal reading.

## 2. The named definitions — philosophy's catalogue

- **British Emergentism** (Mill's heteropathic effects; Lewes coins "emergent" 1875; C. Lloyd Morgan *Emergent Evolution* 1923; **C. D. Broad** *The Mind and Its Place in Nature* 1925): emergent properties are novel, unpredictable-in-principle from the base, governed by irreducible trans-ordinal laws; downward causation typically affirmed. The tradition declined when quantum mechanics explained chemistry (the standing historical example of "emergence" retreating before theory).
- **Chalmers — weak/strong** (2006): as above. Strong emergence entails that physics is not causally closed or that the emergent is epiphenomenal — hence its rarity.
- **Bedau — nominal / weak / strong** (1997, 2002): **nominal** = any macro property a micro constituent cannot have (innocuous, ubiquitous); **weak** = derivable from the micro-dynamics *but only by simulation* (no closed-form shortcut; the crawl-the-micro-steps criterion — the computational sciences' working definition); **strong** = supervenient with irreducible causal powers (Bedau: "uncomfortably like magic" — scientifically irrelevant if real). Wilson's objection: Bedau's weak emergence is epistemic only.
- **Kim — the causal exclusion critique** (1998–2006): if the physical base is causally closed and the emergent supervenes on it, emergent causal powers are either identical to base powers (reduction) or excluded (epiphenomenalism). The modern debate's hinge — every ontological-emergence position answers Kim or fails.
- **Wilson — metaphysical weak/strong via powers** (*Metaphysical Emergence*, 2021): **weak** = the emergent has a *proper subset* of the base's powers (real, metaphysical, compatible with physicalism); **strong** = at least one power *not had* by the base (anti-physicalist). Recasts the whole taxonomy in powers terms; currently the most systematic ontological treatment.
- **Humphreys — fusion emergence** (1997, *Emergence* 2016): base entities *fuse*, losing their identities in the emergent — denies the layered supervenience picture the exclusion argument needs; diachronic and ontological.
- **Van Gulick — the options grid** (2001): a widely-used sorting of reduction/emergence positions into epistemic and ontological variants of ascending strength — the taxonomists' taxonomy.
- **O'Connor — emergent properties as non-structural** (1994 onward): causally basic, macro-level, with genuine downward efficacy; the sustained contemporary defence of something near the British view.

## 3. Emergence per discipline — what the word does where

- **Physics.** Anderson "More is Different" (1972): symmetry breaking; each level with its own laws, "the ability to reduce everything… does not imply the ability to start from those laws and reconstruct the universe." Laughlin (*A Different Universe*, 2005): emergent protectorates — laws insensitive to micro-detail. **Batterman**: universality and renormalisation — the same macro-behaviour across micro-differences as the *mechanism* of level-autonomy; emergence tied to the mathematics of limits. Working sense: mostly **weak/epistemic-plus** — level-autonomy taken as physically serious without further-fact metaphysics. Phase transitions the canonical case.
- **Chemistry.** The textbook register: molecular properties none of the atoms hold (water's liquidity); historically the British Emergentists' home case, then the cautionary tale — quantum mechanics *derived* the chemistry they declared underivable. Live methodological version: whether molecular structure is derivable from quantum mechanics alone (the Born–Oppenheimer/"molecular structure problem" niche).
- **Biology.** Diachronic emergence throughout: origin of life (chemistry → biology — the assembly-theory and origins-of-life literatures), development (form from genome-plus-context), ecosystems (stability and cycling nobody codes). "Property of life" traditions (organicism, autopoiesis) as emergence vocabulary avant la lettre. Mostly weak-emergence usage with periodic strong-emergence rhetoric.
- **Complexity science / ALife / computation.** Emergence as *working tool*, often assumed rather than analysed: Conway's Life (gliders from three rules), Langton's ALife programme, Holland (*Emergence*, 1998 — building blocks and recurring patterns), Goldstein's definitional survey (novelty, coherence, macro-level, dynamical arising, ostensibility). **Bedau's weak emergence is this field's philosophy made explicit** — derivable only by running it. Distinction from **self-organization** (order without external control) — overlapping, not identical: self-organization names the process, emergence the relation of product to base.
- **Social sciences / economics.** The oldest working usage: **Durkheim** — social facts sui generis, exterior and constraining (strong-emergence-flavoured); **Hayek** — spontaneous order (market prices as knowledge no participant holds); **Schelling** — segregation from mild preferences (micro-motives → macro-behaviour, the agent-based-model template); **Sawyer** (*Social Emergence*, 2005) — the explicit application of the philosophy taxonomy to the social. Standing dispute: methodological individualism vs emergent social properties — the social sciences' own exclusion argument.
- **Mind / neuroscience.** The synchronic home case: consciousness as strong emergence's only serious candidate (Chalmers); the field otherwise runs weak-emergence talk about brain dynamics (assemblies, oscillations, workspace ignition). The hard-problem line and the emergence line intersect here and are frequently conflated.
- **AI (current).** "Emergent abilities" of large language models: Wei et al. 2022 — capabilities appearing suddenly at scale; **contested**: Schaeffer et al. 2023 ("a mirage") — the discontinuities largely artifacts of discontinuous metrics; >92% of claimed cases under two such metrics. Status: a live dispute over whether the phenomenon is in the models or in the measuring — a textbook epistemic-vs-ontological instance happening in real time.

## 4. Cross-cutting observations (findings of the mapping, held for the survey)

1. **The axes explain the cross-talk.** Philosophy's centre of gravity is synchronic/ontological; the sciences' is diachronic/epistemic-weak. Much "disagreement about emergence" is two conversations using one word.
2. **Strong emergence has one serious synchronic candidate** (consciousness) and a graveyard (chemistry — derived; vitalism — dissolved). The historical pattern: "in-principle underivable" claims keep becoming derivable. Whether consciousness breaks the pattern is the standing question.
3. **The must-simulate/new-language distinction cuts through every discipline.** Bedau-weak (crawl the dynamics) and new-descriptive-language (a level with its own laws and vocabulary — Anderson, Laughlin) are different findings that the word bundles; the LLM dispute is precisely about which one is being observed.
4. **Downward causation is the recurring colouring candidate**: system-level constraint narrated as the whole *acting on* its parts — flagged across philosophy (Kim's target), social science (structure vs agency), and biology.
5. **Nobody owns a cross-disciplinary definition.** Each discipline's working sense is locally stable; the cross-discipline word is not — which is the finding a language-native reformulation would have to explain rather than lament.
